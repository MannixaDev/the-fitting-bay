The three ways people get this wrong

  • Reaching down. The moment you look at the tape you drop your shoulder and add an inch. Stand still, look ahead, let someone else read it.
  • Measuring in socks. Golf shoes add roughly ½", and you play in golf shoes.
  • Measuring from the wrong point. It is the crease where your palm meets your wrist, not the knuckle, not the base of your thumb, and not the tip of your little finger.
Both numbers move the result. Height sets your club length; height and wrist-to-floor together set your lie angle. A ½" error in wrist-to-floor is enough to change your lie recommendation by a full degree.

The chart everything starts from

The Bay Scale

Our lie-angle scale. Plot your height along the bottom and your wrist-to-floor up the side; where they meet is your static lie angle, and the band along the top gives your club length. The codes say what they mean — U2 is two degrees upright, F1 is one degree flat, LEVEL is standard. Every band is 1" of wrist-to-floor, and the scale runs symmetrically from 5° flat to 5° upright.

Your position is marked once you complete the fitting above. The curve running through the middle of the chart is the reference wrist-to-floor for each height — the line along which a golfer plays standard lie. How far above or below it you sit is your recommendation.
